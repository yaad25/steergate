\title{Does Activation Steering Improve Mathematical Reasoning?\\
A Controlled Replication on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}
\author{yaad25 (h2rn)}
\date{November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Activation steering---adding a direction vector to a language model's residual stream at
inference time---has been proposed as a training-free way to improve multi-step reasoning in
small language models (e.g., DenseSteer; Ouyang et al., 2026). We run a pre-registered,
controlled replication of an on-policy steering recipe on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} and
GSM8K, using a frozen statistical protocol with paired sampling, two independent controls (a
random direction and a shuffled-pair direction, both norm-matched), and a prompt-only baseline.
Across the full 1{,}319-question test set ($k=8$ samples, 10{,}000-resample paired bootstrap),
the steering vector does not improve accuracy over the unsteered model (lift $-1.24$ points, 95\%
CI $[-1.86, -0.64]$) and fails to beat even a random-direction control ($-1.15$ points, 95\% CI
$[-1.77, -0.50]$). A secondary analysis uncovers a hidden $\sim\!4$-point accuracy loss in the
prompt-only baseline from a formatting-compliance bug that disproportionately penalizes dense,
equation-heavy outputs; once corrected, the prompt-only baseline beats steering as well. We
release the full evaluation harness, raw rollout logs, and locked configuration so the result is
independently reproducible. \textbf{Verdict: NO-GO.} This specific recipe does not improve
reasoning accuracy on this model and dataset, and the result is robust to a lenient grading
sensitivity check.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Small language models often under-perform on multi-step arithmetic reasoning relative to their
larger counterparts, even when the underlying knowledge is present. Two broad families of
training-free fixes have been proposed: \textbf{prompting} (asking the model to show dense,
equation-heavy work) and \textbf{activation steering} (nudging the model's internal
representations toward a ``good reasoning'' direction extracted from contrastive examples,
without touching any weights).

Recent work (DenseSteer; Ouyang, Lin, and Kim, 2026) reports that steering a small model's
residual stream toward a ``dense reasoning'' direction---extracted from pairs of verbose vs.\
compressed solution traces---improves GSM8K accuracy by roughly $+2$ points over an unsteered
baseline. Separately, Stanford's AxBench (Wu et al., 2025) found that across a range of
concept-steering tasks, a one-sentence natural-language instruction frequently matches or beats
activation-steering methods, and that increasing the steering strength monotonically degrades
fluency. These two results are in tension: is a small, training-free steering vector a reliable
win, or does it only look that way without a careful prompt baseline and proper noise controls?

Published steering gains on math reasoning are small ($\approx\!+2$ points) and evaluation
protocols in this literature vary widely in sample size, control arms, and grading strictness---
exactly the conditions under which a real effect can be indistinguishable from sampling noise, or
a null effect can look real due to an uncontrolled confound (e.g., answer-formatting compliance).
We set out to test, under a frozen, adversarially-controlled protocol, whether an on-policy
version of this steering recipe reproduces on a popular small open model.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A fully pre-registered, controlled replication protocol for activation steering on math
    reasoning, with two independent null controls and a prompt-only baseline, released as a
    reusable open-source harness (\texttt{steergate\_p0}).
  \item An honest negative result: on-policy dense-reasoning steering does not improve, and in
    fact measurably \emph{hurts}, GSM8K accuracy on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} under our
    protocol.
  \item A methodological finding independent of the steering question: naive strict-format
    grading (requiring a literal \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}} tag) can hide a multi-point
    accuracy difference between arms when one arm's prompting style changes how often the model
    remembers to format its answer---and this bias is \emph{not} uniform across arms, so it can
    silently favor or disfavor whichever arm is being tested.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{DenseSteer} (Ouyang, Lin, and Kim, ICML 2026; arXiv:2605.29247) proposes steering small
models toward a ``dense reasoning'' direction extracted by rewriting a model's own verbose trace
into a compressed one, reporting $\approx\!+2$ point GSM8K gains on Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B. Our
on-policy arm (\texttt{steered\_onpolicy}) directly implements the zero-external-API version of
this recipe.

\textbf{AxBench} (Wu et al., 2025; arXiv:2501.17148) is a large-scale benchmark showing that
simple prompting baselines frequently match or outperform steering-vector methods, and that
steering strength has a fluency/efficacy trade-off. Motivates our \texttt{prompt\_dense} baseline
arm and our use of an explicit fluency/truncation guard.

\textbf{Contrastive Activation Addition / Representation Engineering} (Rimsky et al.; Zou et al.)
establish the mean-difference extraction method we use (CAA) and the broader linear
representation hypothesis underlying steering.

\textbf{Arditi et al.} (refusal direction) show that \emph{directional ablation} of a single
linear direction can remove a specific behavior (refusal) without retraining---the same family of
technique as the ``abliteration'' used elsewhere in this project, and conceptually related to our
random/shuffled control construction.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Model and task}
\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, evaluated on the full GSM8K test set (1{,}319 questions).
Generation: temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.8$, $k=8$ samples per question, max 1024 new tokens,
repetition penalty $1.0$. Sampling uses a step-indexed Gumbel-max common-random-number scheme so
that every arm sees \emph{identical} sampling noise for a given (question, sample) pair,
independent of batch size---this removes a major source of unpaired variance between arms.

\subsection{Three-way disjoint data split}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Extraction}: \texttt{GSM8K-train[0:150]}, used to build the on-policy contrastive
    trace pairs (118 kept, filtered to cases the model itself answered correctly).
  \item \textbf{Tune}: a held-out slice of \texttt{GSM8K-train} plus 50 WikiText-2 passages, used
    once, before the test set was ever touched, to sweep candidate layers $\{12,14,16,18,20\}
    \times \alpha \in \{1.0, 1.5\}$ under a KL-divergence fluency guard (KL $\le 0.15$
    nats/token vs.\ unsteered) and a truncation-rate guard. \textbf{Locked result: layer 16,
    $\alpha = 1.5$.}
  \item \textbf{Test}: the full 1{,}319-question GSM8K test set, untouched until this evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vector construction}
For each of 118 kept trace pairs, the model's own verbose (``sparse'') solution is
deterministically compressed into a dense version (filler sentences stripped, equations and the
final boxed answer kept)---zero external API calls. The steering vector is the normalized mean
difference between dense- and sparse-trace activations at the locked layer, scaled to a dosage
matching the locked $\alpha$ on each token's residual-stream norm.

\subsection{Arms}
All five arms are paired on identical sampling noise.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Arm & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{base} & Unsteered model, standard CoT instruction \\
\texttt{prompt\_dense} & Unsteered model, instructed to show explicit equations every step \\
\texttt{steered\_onpolicy} & Base instruction + the locked on-policy steering vector \\
\texttt{ctrl\_random} & Base instruction + a random direction, norm-matched to the real vector \\
\texttt{ctrl\_shuffled} & Base instruction + a vector from the same pairs with flipped labels \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

A sixth arm (\texttt{steered\_teacher}, using a frontier-model-rewritten trace as in the original
DenseSteer recipe) was planned but cut: it requires an external LLM API we did not have access
to. This is a scope limitation, documented rather than silently dropped.

\subsection{Statistics and the frozen decision rule}
For each arm, per-question accuracy is the mean correctness over its $k=8$ samples. For a given
comparison, we compute the paired per-question difference and run a 10{,}000-sample
question-clustered bootstrap to get a point estimate and 95\%/one-sided-95\% confidence
intervals. The decision rule, frozen before any steered result was seen:

\textbf{GO} requires the steered arm's lower 95\% CI bound to exceed 0 against all three of
\texttt{base}, \texttt{ctrl\_random}, and \texttt{ctrl\_shuffled} simultaneously, with point lift
$\ge +1.0$ points. \textbf{NO-GO} if the one-sided 95\% upper bound is below $+1.0$, or the arm
fails to beat either control's point estimate. \textbf{BROKEN} if truncation or box-parse failure
rate rises more than 1 point above base (none of our arms triggered this).

Under the frozen rule, a single failed comparison against base or either control is sufficient
for an overall NO-GO. Steering fails against \textbf{base} and \textbf{ctrl\_random}, with

is a \emph{significant negative} effect.

\subsection{Secondary finding: a formatting-compliance confound}

While inspecting failure cases, we found instances where the model produced the exactly correct
final number but never wrapped it in \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}, causing the strict grader
to mark a correct answer as wrong. We quantified this across all five arms:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Arm & \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}} missing & Hidden accuracy loss \\
\midrule
base & 1.36\% & 0.60 pts \\
\textbf{prompt\_dense} & \textbf{5.40\%} & \textbf{3.20 pts} \\
steered\_onpolicy & 1.81\% & 0.97 pts \\
ctrl\_random & 1.10\% & 0.48 pts \\
ctrl\_shuffled & 1.91\% & 1.00 pts \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The bug disproportionately affects \texttt{prompt\_dense}. Re-grading all five arms with a
lenient parser (credit a correct final number even without a box) and recomputing the same four
comparisons:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Comparison (lenient grading) & Lift & 95\% CI & Decision \\
\midrule
vs.\ base & $-0.87$ pts & $[-1.45, -0.27]$ & \textbf{NO-GO} \\
vs.\ prompt\_dense & $-0.88$ pts & $[-1.74, -0.01]$ & \textbf{NO-GO (flipped from GO)} \\
vs.\ ctrl\_random & $-0.64$ pts & $[-1.27, -0.03]$ & \textbf{NO-GO} \\
vs.\ ctrl\_shuffled & $-0.44$ pts & $[-0.94, +0.07]$ & \textbf{NO-GO} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Under lenient grading, steering loses to \emph{every single arm}, including \texttt{prompt\_dense}.
The strict-grading ``win'' against \texttt{prompt\_dense} was entirely an artifact of unfairly
penalizing the prompt baseline's formatting habits, not a genuine steering advantage.

\section{Discussion}

Why might on-policy steering fail here where DenseSteer reports a gain? Several
non-exclusive possibilities, none of which we can distinguish with this dataset alone: (1)
\textbf{trace quality}---our on-policy traces are self-compressed with zero external
supervision, whereas DenseSteer's main numbers may rely on teacher-rewritten traces (our
\texttt{steered\_teacher} arm, which would test this directly, was cut for lack of API access);
(2) \textbf{harness/prompt differences}---GSM8K scoring is known to be sensitive to prompt
template and extraction method across papers; (3) \textbf{a genuinely small or absent effect}
for this specific layer/scale/model combination, given that published steering gains in this
space are already close to the noise floor. We do not claim to have falsified DenseSteer's
reported result---only that this specific, carefully-controlled on-policy replication on this
model does not reproduce a positive effect, and in fact shows a significant negative one against
two independent null controls.

\subsection*{Limitations}
The teacher-distilled arm was cut (no external LLM API available). The hyperparameter sweep in
the tune phase was trimmed under same-day time pressure ($N=80$ tune questions,
$\alpha \in \{1.0, 1.5\}$ instead of the originally planned $N=200$,
$\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$); a wider sweep might locate a better-performing
(layer, $\alpha$) pair. This is a documented deviation, not a silent one. We test a single model
and a single dataset; we make no claim about transfer to other model families, sizes, or
reasoning benchmarks.

\section{Conclusion}

Under a pre-registered, adversarially-controlled replication protocol---paired sampling, two
independent null controls, and a prompt-only baseline---on-policy dense-reasoning activation
steering does not improve GSM8K accuracy on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, and significantly
\emph{underperforms} both the unsteered model and a random-direction control. This result is
stable under a lenient grading sensitivity check, which additionally reveals that a
formatting-compliance confound can mask a multi-point accuracy difference in strict-grading
comparisons. We release the full harness, locked configuration, and raw per-question logs so
this result can be independently verified, extended to other models, or falsified.
